Predicting NHL Player Salary Valuation

R-StudioRegression AnalysisCross-ValidationSports AnalyticsRandom Forest

This project identifies the best predictive method for estimating NHL player salaries using a dataset of 874 players from the 2016–17 season, sourced from Hockey Abstract via Kaggle. After cleaning, the working dataset contained 364 observations across 153 input variables.

The study compared linear regression (OLS), polynomial regression, step functions, Lasso, regression trees, and random forests. Each model was evaluated using split-half cross-validation to estimate out-of-sample test MSE. Key predictors included games played, points scored, time on ice, primary assists, and defensive point shares.

Step functions on the points variable produced the lowest estimated test MSE of 2.644 × 10¹², outperforming all other methods. The final model was also used to assess which players are over or underpaid relative to their predicted market value, with implications for team salary cap management and player contract negotiations.

002

Age & Soccer Player Market Value

R-StudioSTATAOLS RegressionProbitEconometricsNon-Linear Regression

This econometric study examines how age shapes a professional soccer player's current market value, using a dataset of 9,525 outfield players from 20 leagues across the 2021–22 and 2022–23 seasons.

Multiple regression techniques were applied including OLS, quadratic regression with interaction terms, and probit regression. The dependent variable was modeled both as a continuous market value and as a binary indicator of whether the player exceeds the median value of €900,000.

Key findings: a one-year increase in age is associated with an average decrease of approximately €212,000 in market value after controlling for goals, assists, and awards. Probit results show that players under 25 see increasing probability of being above the median value as they age, while players over 30 see a declining probability. The study concludes that age is a statistically significant predictor of market value, with non-linear dynamics that vary by career stage.

Tunisia Market & Financial Analysis

Macroeconomic AnalysisFX & Currency RiskTrade AnalysisPortfolio DiversificationFinancial Sector

A comprehensive market study of Tunisia evaluating its investment potential for foreign investors. The analysis covers market openness via Trade-to-GDP ratios, sector concentration, exchange rate dynamics under the IMF-classified crawl-like arrangement, and financial sector strength.

Tunisia's currency has depreciated significantly against the USD — from 1.96 dinars per dollar in 2015 to over 3.1 by recent years — driven by the Arab Spring, trade deficits, and high public debt at 80% of GDP. The study analyzed monthly exchange rate data from 2000–2024 alongside interest rate differentials to identify carry trade opportunities.

Despite structural challenges including state-dominated banking, regulatory complexity, and a CPI corruption score of 44/100, Tunisia's negative correlation with US equity returns (−0.47) and Sharpe ratio dynamics support selective portfolio diversification. The report concludes with a risk-tiered investment framework covering FDI, equities, government bonds, currency exposure, and real estate.

Big Mac Index & Global Export Analysis

R / dplyrggplot2PPP TheoryFeature EngineeringTrade AnalysisData Visualization

A team data visualization project testing the validity of Purchasing Power Parity theory using The Economist's Big Mac Index as a PPP proxy across 50+ countries from 2000–2022.

Big Mac price data was merged with World Bank export, import, GDP, and population figures. Key engineered features included trade balance, exports as a share of GDP, and exports per capita. All data wrangling and visualization were done in R using dplyr, tidyr, and ggplot2.

Key finding: countries with undervalued currencies (per the Big Mac Index) tend to show stronger export competitiveness, with Singapore and Malaysia as standout cases. A notable 2011 spike in exports per capita across undervalued Asian economies was traced to post-recession demand rebounds. The study provides a visual framework for understanding currency mispricing and its real trade effects.

008

Cancer Clinical Trial Patient Selection

K-Means ClusteringPCAkNN Outlier DetectionIsolation ForestR ProgrammingClinical Analytics

Michigan Medicine is piloting a costly cancer treatment where each clinical trial slot carries significant medical and financial weight. The analytical challenge: identify true cancer patients in an unlabeled 378-patient dataset while maximizing precision and holding false positives to an absolute minimum.

The approach combined four unsupervised learning methods. PCA reduced the dataset to 12 principal components capturing 97% of variance. kNN outlier detection, Isolation Forest, and k-means clustering were then applied separately, and their outputs were combined using an ensemble rule tuned to hit the minimum 7 true positive threshold at maximum precision.

The final combined model achieved Precision of 0.467, Recall of 0.333, and Specificity of 0.978 — correctly identifying 7 true cancer patients while keeping false positives low enough to protect limited trial resources. This project demonstrates how unsupervised ML can be applied rigorously to high-stakes clinical decision-making.

Detroit Police Crime Response Analysis

TableauData VisualizationPublic PolicyEquity AnalysisGeospatial AnalysisGeospatial Analysis

This team project investigates whether Detroit Police deliver equal protection across all neighborhoods, using a dataset of police call records merged with population data, housing values, and zip code demographics.

Using Tableau, the analysis visualized average response times (ART) and calls per 1,000 residents by zip code, revealing a counterintuitive pattern: the highest call-volume zip codes per capita actually received the fastest response times. Zip codes with the highest ARTs — 48240, 48239, and 48236 — showed disproportionately slow responses despite similar call types and priority levels to lower-ART areas.

Disturbance calls dominated across all zip codes, but volume disparities were stark. Peak disturbance hours revealed concentrated resource gaps in specific neighborhoods. The team recommended a review of staffing allocation by time of day and geography, priority level reassignment practices, and city funding reallocation to address systemic response time inequities across Detroit's communities.
